V3-SG: what gets trained, and how the analysis works

A plain reading of docs/VERSION3_SUBGRID_MODEL_IDEA.md. Three diagrams: the training sequence, the generator, and data assimilation.

1The idea in one paragraph

The old model predicted 5 km rainfall in one shot from 50 km inputs, so there was no way to tell whether its fine detail was real. V3-SG splits the job in two: how much rain falls in each 0.5° box, and where inside the box it lands. Splitting it makes the second half separately testable — and it lets each observation act at the scale it can actually see. IMERG measures 0.4° area means, so it should mostly move amounts; a rain gauge measures one point, so it should mostly move placement.

2What actually gets trained

Three networks, but only the third one is used operationally. The first two exist to be trained cheaply, diagnosed separately, and then folded into the third.

PieceGridWhat it learnsUsed for
Model A0.5°
(14×13)		 How much rain in each CPC box, and whether it rained at all. A rectified flow over two latents — a wetness logit and a positive-amount latent — decoded by a hurdle rule so exact zeros are possible. Diagnosis, and the starting weights for the coupled flow's coarse branch.
Model B0.05°
(140×130)		 Given a box total, how to split it across the 100 cells inside. Outputs per-cell wetness logits and relative weights — never a total. The oracle experiments, and the starting weights for the fine branch.
Coupled flowboth Both at once, as one joint state. This is the operational model. Generating the background, and supplying the prior score during DA.
R(m, z)— Not learned. A fixed decoder that turns (amount, allocation) into mm/day, guaranteeing each box's area-weighted mean equals its amount exactly. Every arm, every phase.

Why a third model rather than just running A then B? Guiding both amount and allocation with observations needs the gradient of the joint prior. Two separately-trained models give you ∇m log p(m) and ∇z log p(z|m), but not ∇m log p(z|m) — the term saying "changing the amount makes this allocation more or less plausible." Dropping it lets a gauge push a box heavy while the allocation stays one that only makes sense for a light box. The coupled flow produces both velocity components from one network, so the term is there by construction.

5Data assimilation

Same model, now pulled toward observations while it samples. Nothing is retrained.

The analysis state is the pair (m, z), not the rainfall field. Observations are compared against rainfall, so each one is mapped through the same decoder — and the gradient flows back to whichever half of the state can explain it.

The part that carries the scientific claim

The labels "mostly moves m" and "mostly moves z" are expectations, not wiring. Nothing in the design forces IMERG to leave placement alone — the coupled prior is free to trade between amount and allocation however it learned to. So where each observation's influence actually lands is measured, by rebuilding the rainfall field four ways from the background and analysis halves and splitting the difference:

  • C_m — the part of the change explained by box amounts moving
  • C_z — the part explained by rain being repositioned inside boxes
  • they add back to the full increment exactly, so nothing is unaccounted for

This is run for satellite-only, gauge-only, and both together, plus a controlled experiment that pokes a single IMERG footprint and a single gauge with the same size innovation and compares. If the split does not come out as expected, the design still works as a rainfall model — but the "division of observational authority" claim comes out of the paper.

Why 0.5° and 0.4° deliberately don't match. The prior conserves on CPC's native 0.5° boxes; IMERG is assimilated on its own 0.4° footprints. Because they are offset, a satellite footprint spans parts of several boxes — so satisfying it can require moving rain around inside boxes, not just scaling them. That mismatch is what makes the amount/placement division something to measure rather than something true by definition.

6What gets compared against what

Eleven arms, but they answer four questions in sequence.

QuestionCompare
Is there any daily signal?oracle flow vs. a monthly climatological split of the same box totals
What did coupling cost?Model B oracle vs. coupled flow with amounts pinned to truth
What did real amounts cost?coupled oracle vs. the full operational background
Does DA help?each observation arm vs. the background, and vs. the frozen v2 baseline

Every comparison gives both sides the same box amounts, so the score isolates placement rather than rewarding a better total. Scoring is CRPS computed member by member, which keeps a blurry ensemble from winning by averaging.
