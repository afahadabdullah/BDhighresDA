One variable,
changed on purpose.

G0 is not a graph weather model. It is eight rounds of message passing inserted inside the existing CPCv2 U-Net, at encoder level 2, on a 32×32 latent grid — zero-initialised so that at step zero it produces bit-identical output. Everything else in the system is frozen, which is the only reason the comparison means anything.

The real topology, built in your browser from the same construction rule as src/bdhires/models/multimesh.py: at stride s both endpoints lie on rows and columns that are multiples of s, connected by (0,s), (s,0), (s,s), (s,−s) and their reverses, with no periodic wrap and no self-edges. It reproduces the documented counts exactly: 1,024 nodes and 10,176 directed edges.

01

What G0 changes — and everything it does not

The scientific question is narrow and worth stating precisely: does explicit multiscale communication improve the learned precipitation prior and its observation-response structure, under the same generative data assimilation? Broader sensitivity on its own is not evidence of a better covariance — unphysical distant responses would count against the architecture, not for it.

Changed

+A MultimeshProcessor at encoder level 2: 288 latent channels projected to a 256-dim hidden state, 8 residual message-passing blocks, projected back and added to the incoming latent field.
+Latent grid dimensions follow the input shape divided by four, not a hard-coded resolution — rectangular canvases still work.
+The output projection is zero-initialised, so identical U-Net weights give exactly the same initial output. The graph still executes; it is not a bypassed branch.
+3,906,080 parameters, a 6.8% increase.

Frozen — and a test enforces it

=The rectified-flow equations, the score/velocity conversion, the likelihood, observation operators, Heun integration, perturbations and Langevin correctors. flow.py, guidance.py, observation.py, sampler.py untouched.
=The same packed Zarr and stats_cpc_v2.json, CHIRPS target, square-root transforms, and the standardised residual against CPC.
=Seven dynamic channels, seven static, two seasonal. Training 1981–2018, validation 2019–2020, test 2021–2025. Batch 32, 150 epochs, EMA 0.9995, dropout 0.2, wet sampling, coarse consistency.
=BMD and IMERG never become learned inputs or graph nodes. They remain likelihood observations — which is what permits assimilation without retraining.

That last point separates G0 sharply from the graph-diffusion systems it superficially resembles. There, observations are nodes and the observation operator is learned. Here the graph sees only static latent geometry — [dx, dy, distance, level] — and time and meteorological conditioning arrive through the CNN that surrounds it. No second time encoder, no duplicated feature stack, no wind-dependent topology.

02

The multimesh, as actually constructed

Four nested strides over the same 32×32 latent grid. Stride 1 connects every node to its eight neighbours; stride 8 connects a sparse skeleton with edges that jump eight latent cells at once. Because one latent cell here is four fine cells — 0.2°, about 22 km — a single stride-8 hop carries information roughly 178 km, and four of them cross the whole 710 km domain. That is the architectural argument in physical units: the CNN would need many layers to do the same.

03

Inside one message block

Eight identical blocks, each a residual update. Two implementation decisions in here are the difference between a graph layer that fits in memory during guided assimilation and one that does not.

normalise  h = LayerNorm(v)
message    me = MLP( Wsh[srce] + Wdh[dste] + Wgge )
aggregate  aj = ( Σe : dst=j me ) / deg(j)
update     v' = v + MLP( [ h , a ] )
static edge features, never updated
ge = MLP( [ dx/smax , dy/smax , dist/smax , level/L ] )

Why it is written that way

The first edge linear map is factored. A linear layer on the concatenation [v_src, v_dst, g] would allocate a [B, E, 3D] tensor — with E = 10,176 and D = 256 that is the memory wall. Projecting the three terms separately and adding after the gather is algebraically identical and never materialises it.

Edge hidden states do not evolve. The geometry embedding is computed once and reused by all eight blocks. Nodes carry the state; edges carry only the static relation. That halves what the backward pass has to keep.

Aggregation is a degree-normalised mean, accumulated in FP32 even under BF16 autocast, and in FP64 when a double-precision gradient check is running. Incoming degree ranges from 3 at a corner to 32 in the interior, so an unnormalised sum would make the boundary a different operator from the middle.

The topology itself is cached in a small per-processor LRU of at most four shape/topology/device combinations. It is deliberately absent from state_dict, EMA and checkpoints, cleared when the model moves device, and a cache miss under inference_mode builds normal tensors on purpose — so that a later guided call can save the same indices for the autograd backward pass.

04

What it costs

Measured at 128×128, batch 1, 16 conditioning channels, FP32 on a macOS ARM CPU with four threads — one warmup and three timed repeats, medians. Desktop timings are noisy and establish nothing about HPC throughput, but the shape of the result is the useful part.

The asymmetry is the point. Training gets 36% slower, but the guided gradient — the thing that runs thirty members across fifty Heun steps for every fold of every day — costs 2.1% more. The benchmark includes the real coarse-consistency loss and the existing physical gauge and block-average likelihood with component spreading, so it is not a toy measurement. Its synthetic error vector is a controlled cost benchmark, not an IMERG error calibration.

CUDA performance and memory, batch 32, and 30-member DA have not been verified. The measured CPU guidance ratio is below the 1.5× engineering target, but a GPU OOM at batch 32 would be recorded explicitly rather than silently worked around by shrinking the batch.

05

A better prior did not give a better analysis

The matched comparison ran four cases — CPCv2 background and DA, GraphFlow background and DA — over five identical spatial folds, 1–10 May 2022, 30 members, with the frozen v2_simul_s04_ig010 settings copied under new names so neither prior got a tuned advantage. The clean test is not which analysis scores better. It is the difference in within-model DA gain: does GraphFlow extract more useful information from the observations, or does it merely start from a better place?

The result, stated at the confidence the experiment supports
Better

The background improved significantly

The unguided GraphFlow prior beat the CPCv2 prior on the matched folds. Explicit multiscale communication did something for the learned field.

Worse

The analysis was underdispersed

A sharper prior with the same likelihood authority collapses further. Spread that the old contract was calibrated against is no longer there to give away.

Less

Smaller incremental DA benefit

The unchanged CPCv2 guidance extracted less additional value from the same observations. The DA contract was tuned for the old prior, not this one.

1–10 May 2022 is the existing development and selection window, which is exactly why it is excluded from the frozen production aggregates. This is a controlled diagnostic, not an independent confirmation, and no learned-checkpoint CRPS or RMSE gain is claimed anywhere in the repository.

Which is why the next step is not a better backbone but a better contract. A broad one-fold screen puts 49 assimilation arms plus one common background around the frozen baseline, each changing a single named mechanism. Pick one to see what it does.

Known limitations of G0 specifically

It augments the U-Net and keeps its attention, so it is not compute-matched — some of any gain may be the extra 6.8% of parameters rather than the graph. There is no learned coarsening and no geographical metric; edges are latent-grid geometry. CUDA scatter reductions can differ from CPU at floating-point roundoff, so seed reproducibility is not a promise of cross-device bitwise identity.

What comes after

G1 would replace the now-redundant attention at matched compute — which is the experiment that would actually separate "the graph helps" from "more parameters help". G2 would investigate atmospheric edge features instead of pure geometry. G3 would compare a different hierarchy. None of them are implemented, and none should start before the five-fold DA comparison closes.

This page belongs to a set of three. The SURMA-Flow explainer covers the production rectified-flow U-Net and the score-guided DA that G0 leaves entirely untouched; the graph-diffusion page covers the very different design — global mesh, observations as nodes, a learned observation operator — that G0 is sometimes mistaken for.
