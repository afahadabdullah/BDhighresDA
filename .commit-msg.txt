Build the scale ladder from coarsened observation files, not a config flag

The S04 and S08 arms died before their first day with

    ValueError: operands could not be broadcast together with shapes (64,) (16,)

observations.imerg.factor is not an operator knob. scripts/15 builds
grid.lat[:n*factor].reshape(n,factor).mean(1) and requires the observation
FILE's own latitudes to equal it, so factor declares which grid the file is
on: the model grid is 128x128 at 0.05 deg, the prepared IMERG 64x64 at 0.1,
and factor 8/16 demanded 16x16 and 8x8 files that did not exist. The guard's
own message never printed because np.allclose raises on mismatched shapes
before it can return False.

scripts/44 block-averages the prepared file onto the coarse footprints. Three
things move with the factor, and each one alone would make the arm measure
something other than observation scale:

  - randomError cannot be averaged as though footprints were independent.
    Var(block mean) = (1/n^2) sum_ij rho_ij s_i s_j; under the exponential
    correlation implied by error_corr_cells the 4x4 block keeps sigma x0.744,
    not the x0.25 of s/sqrt(n). Assuming independence would have overstated
    the satellite's precision by 3.0x at 0.4 deg and 4.5x at 0.8.
  - error_corr_cells is denominated in cells OF THE OBSERVATION GRID, so the
    configured 3.0 means 0.30 deg at 0.1 deg but would assert 1.2 deg at 0.4.
    Holding the physical length fixed gives 0.75 and 0.375. The length is
    derived from the configured cell count rather than a second constant, so
    the file and configs/da.yaml cannot drift apart.
  - stride must drop to 1. Thinning exists to decorrelate and coarsening
    already does that; stride 3 would leave 25 footprints at 0.4 deg and 4
    at 0.8, which is not an experiment.

The block mean is exact and mass-conserving, and the coarse centres nest on
the model grid by construction. Verified against scripts/15's real
load_prepared_imerg, extracted with ast: both scales are accepted, the
centres equal grid.lat[:n*factor].reshape(n,factor).mean(1) to 1e-6, the
domain mean is unchanged, and the error scale stays strictly between the
independent and fully-correlated bounds. Ragged, non-multiple and
non-coarsening factors are refused.

The nine arms already running are untouched -- they all use factor 2. Rerun
with ARMS="S04 S08" to add the ladder.
